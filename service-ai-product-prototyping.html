Behaviour you can try, not a document you read

A spec document versus a working prototype — and why only one actually shows you how the feature behaves.

A spec document

“The assistant should summarise the thread.”

A sentence that sounds reasonable — until you see what “summarise” actually produces, how long it takes, and whether users trust the result. The doc can't answer any of that.

A working prototype

“Here's the summary — react to it.”

We prototype the interface and the AI's expected responses side by side, so you can see how a feature really behaves and put it in front of real users early — before full development starts.

Learn what's wrong in days, not after launch

Four ways a working prototype catches what a spec never could, before a line of production code exists.

Test before you build. Validate whether an AI feature actually works for users before committing engineering resources to it — the prototype is the cheap version of finding out, not the expensive one.

See real behaviour, not a spec. A working prototype shows how the AI actually responds, tone and timing included — a document describing what it "should" do can't, because a paragraph has never once caught an awkward response.

Catch problems early. Unclear flows, confusing responses, and trust issues surface while the prototype is still cheap to change, not after launch when the fix means a re-release.

Faster iteration. Prototypes can be revised in days, not sprints, so you can test three different tones of voice or fallback behaviours before committing engineering time to just one.

How we prototype without overbuilding

Three ways we keep a prototype honest about what it can and can't tell you.

01

Interface and AI together

We prototype the responses alongside the screens, never in isolation, so the interaction and the interface get validated together.

02

Flows that matter first

Start with the flows that matter most, not every edge case, at the lowest fidelity that still answers the actual question.

03

Real users, early

We put it in front of people outside the building, not just internal demos, before treating any answer as validated.
