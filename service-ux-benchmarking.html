UX benchmarking

We help teams understand their users through numbers and through conversations. One tells you what's happening. The other tells you why.

Benchmark your product

Two ways of understanding users

What UX benchmarking measures, and the four metrics that combine into a single index score.

Definition

“Are we better than last release?”

UX benchmarking evaluates your experience with a consistent set of metrics, measured against a meaningful standard — a previous version, a competitor, or an industry baseline. The result is a single UX Index: one number you can track release over release.

What feeds the index
Task success rate
How many people complete the core task, unaided.
Time on task
How long the task takes against the standard.
Error rate
How often people slip, backtrack, or hit a dead end.
Satisfaction
A standardised score (SUS / CSAT) for how it felt.

The UX Index, release over release

One score, tracked at every major release, plotted against the standard you chose. A redesign should move this line up — benchmarking is how you prove it did.

  • Your product
  • Industry baseline

Proof your experience is improving — not just changing

Three guardrails that keep the index honest, release after release.

Consistent metrics. The same measures, captured the same way each time — that's what makes scores comparable release over release, instead of each audit inventing its own yardstick.

A clear standard. Measured against a previous release, a competitor, or an industry baseline, chosen up front, so every score means the same thing to everyone reading it later.

Task-based, not opinion. Scored on whether people complete the core task, unaided — not on a reviewer's opinion of the interface, which is exactly the subjectivity benchmarking exists to remove.
