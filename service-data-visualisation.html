Data visualisation

We turn complex data into charts, dashboards, and visuals people can actually read at a glance — not just accurate, but understandable.

Visualise your data

From spreadsheet to a glance

A table of numbers against a well-made chart — and why only one lets the pattern find the reader.

A table of numbers

“Read every row to find the problem.”

Accurate, complete — and slow. A grid of figures makes the reader do the work of scanning, comparing, and spotting the pattern themselves.

A well-made chart

“See the problem in a second.”

Data visualisation represents data visually — charts, graphs, maps, dashboards — so it can be understood quickly instead of parsed line by line. The pattern comes to the reader.

Let people see it, not read it

Four reasons seeing the pattern beats reading the numbers that make it.

Faster comprehension. A well-designed chart communicates a pattern instantly — a table of numbers takes far longer to parse, and a stakeholder skimming a report on their phone will read the shape, not the digits.

Clearer decisions. When users can see the data instead of reading it, they make faster, more confident decisions — the funnel drop shows up as one steep bar, not a column of percentages someone has to subtract in their head.

Scales to audiences. The same data can be shown at a high level for casual users or with detail for experts, without redesigning from scratch — a summary chart and its drill-down share one dataset, not two disconnected builds.

Reduces cognitive load. Good visualisation does the work of finding the pattern, so the user doesn't have to — the highlight is chosen for them instead of left for them to hunt down row by row.

How a chart earns its place on the page

From picking the right chart for the audience to making sure everyone can actually read it.

01

Know the audience first

We choose the chart form for who's actually reading it and what they need to decide, before anything else about the data.

02

Simplicity over decoration

Every visual element earns its place on the chart — nothing sits there purely to look impressive or fill space.

03

Right chart for the data

The chart form fits what the data actually has to show, never picked because it looks more impressive on a slide.

04

Consistent colour and scale

Related visuals share the same colour and scale encoding, so a reader can compare them without relearning the key.

05

Drill-down when needed

Interactive detail sits one click away for readers who need to go deeper, without cluttering the view for everyone else.

06

Accessible by default

Readable for colour-blind and low-vision users from the start, not patched in with a filter after the fact.
