UX audit

A structured review of your product against usability heuristics — what's broken, how bad it is, and what to fix first.

Audit your product

Diagnosis, not opinion

What a UX audit is, and the four heuristics every screen gets checked against.

Definition

“Where is this actually breaking?”

A UX audit is a structured, expert-led review of your product against established usability heuristics — navigation, consistency, error handling, accessibility. It doesn't need new users or new research, just a trained eye and a clear framework. The output is a ranked list of issues, not a vague impression.

What we check
Navigation & findability
Can people get where they're going without hunting?
Consistency
Do the same elements behave the same way everywhere?
Error prevention & recovery
Does the product stop mistakes, and help people undo them?
Accessibility
Does it work by keyboard, with a screen reader, at low vision?

Cheaper than finding out the hard way

Four reasons a heuristic review earns its place before a deeper research or redesign budget.

Fast, no recruiting. Runs on your existing product, not a study that takes weeks to schedule and staff — there's no participant pool to build, just a screen to look at.

Ranked, not just noted. Every finding is scored by severity, so you know what to fix first, not just what's wrong — a broken checkout step outranks a slightly-off icon every time.

Catches the obvious first. Clears the heuristic-level issues before you spend a research budget rediscovering them — nobody needs five user interviews to learn a form has no error states.

A second opinion. An outside, trained eye catches what a team too close to the product stops seeing, the same way you stop noticing a typo you've read past fifty times.

How we keep a finding fair

Every issue traced to a named heuristic and a fix, not just a screenshot with a complaint.

01

Heuristic, not opinion

Every finding is traced back to a named usability principle, not a personal preference dressed up as expertise.

02

Severity, not a wall of notes

Each issue is scored — critical, major, minor — so priority is obvious at a glance, not buried in a long list.

03

A fix, not just a flag

Every finding ships with a recommended direction, not just a description of what's wrong on the screen.
