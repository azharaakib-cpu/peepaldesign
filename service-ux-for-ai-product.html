UX for AI products

AI behaves differently from traditional software — probabilistic, sometimes wrong, hard to predict. We design for that: control, transparency, and trust built into every interaction.

Design for AI trust

Designing for systems that guess

AI products predict, recommend, or generate rather than follow fixed rules. The interface has to do more than traditional software — because the output can be wrong.

Traditional software

“Same input, same output.”

Deterministic and predictable. The interface can assume the system did exactly what it was told, so it mostly needs to stay out of the way.

AI product

“Probably right — here's why.”

Probabilistic and uncertain. The interface has to show what the system is doing, let users correct it, and set the right expectations before something goes wrong.

Trust has to be designed in

Users won't rely on an AI feature they don't understand or can't predict.

Errors work differently with AI. It fails unpredictably, not like a traditional bug — the interface needs fallback paths and clear recovery, not just an error message.

Control keeps users confident. Letting people edit, override, or regenerate output matters more than getting it right every time — it's what makes an occasional wrong guess tolerable.

Explainability builds adoption. Showing why a recommendation or score appeared turns a black box into something users will actually act on, instead of second-guessing.
